Ten α+α elastic candidates from run 128, clustered into a beam track (amber) and two arms (blue, green) leaving a fitted vertex (red ring). Every dot is one pad hit; positions are Lorentz-corrected. Use the frame button to see why the tracks look tilted: in the detector frame the cylinder is straight and the beam is inclined; in the solenoid frame the beam is straight and the detector is tilted — which is the physical situation, since the TPC is mounted at an angle inside the magnet. Nothing about the data changes, only the frame.

Three things that surprise people.
The beam is not along z at x=y=0 — it is tilted ~6.4°, within ~12 mm of the axis near the entrance but ~95 mm off by the pad plane.

The vertex is not at (0,0) — vertices sit a median 50 mm off axis, because the beam is itself offset and has a finite spot.

The arms are not back-to-back. Equal-mass elastic gives a 90° opening in the lab; 180° is the centre-of-mass picture.

Run 128, branch FairRootv18.00-fairroot19-port. Events are clustered with direction-aware HDBSCAN and kept only if the opening angle falls in the 80–100° elastic band: scattering off the C and O in the CO₂ populates 60–70° and would confuse the topology. Vertices are fitted from all three segments. De-tilting uses the beam axis measured at B = 0 in run 100 (5.44°, −173.2°), not the parameter file's TiltAng/ThetaRot. The rotation pivots at the beam entrance on the axis, (0, 0, 1000) mm: the angle is the same for any pivot, but the pad-plane centre would push the de-tilted beam 103 mm off axis instead of 43. The grey cylinder is the 1 m active volume: the beam enters at z≈1000 mm and drifts toward the pad plane at z≈0.
